# Aircraft notes

> **JavaScript port (2026-11-19):** written for the Godot version, so file names below (`.gd`, `.tres`, `res://`) are the old ones. The behaviour and numbers still apply. Now: aircraft data in `src/data/aircraft.js` (snake_case fields are camelCase), sprites in `public/sprites/` (unchanged), tools write there, drawing in `src/render/renderer.js`. Cockpit panels: `src/ui/cockpit.js`. Full map: `TECH.md` "Godot to JS map".

The flyable aircraft, their data and art, the sprite tool, ranks and lights. Moved from CLAUDE.md "Working notes" (2026-11-18); keep up to date.

A softer version (20/60/22° + a fly-by-wire G limit) was tried and **rejected by the owner as "unnecessarily smooth"**; keep the crisp numbers. Nose gear sits under the intake, not the nose.
- **Moving control surfaces:** F-16 all-moving tailplane is a separate layer (`f16_elevator.png`, `f16a_elevator.png`) cut from the art inside the sprite tool's `elevator.poly`, drawn rotated about `sprite_elevator_hinge` by `elevator_max_deg` (20) x `Aircraft.elevator` (follows the stick at `elevator_speed`). Trailing edge up for nose-up. **What's under the tailplane** (shows when it rotates): each F-16's body is the owner's picture of the same art WITHOUT the tailplane (`docs/reference/f16a_no_tailplane_source.png`, `f16_no_tailplane_source.png`; sprite tool `elevator.under_src` + `under_offset`), the tailplane layer is cut from the normal art inside `elevator.poly`. F-16A normal art: `f16a_with_tailplane_source.png` (same art as the old `f16a_sprite_source.png`, shifted (15, 4)); F-16D: `f16_sprite_source.png` (identical to the owner's with-tailplane picture). The D's gear areas are cut from its no-tailplane picture too (gear is its own layer). History (2026-11-18): grey fill + mirrored nozzle, then borrowing the A's art for the D, both looked wrong; the owner then supplied the no-tailplane pictures. Mushshak: none (owner: skip for now).
- **Cockpit per aircraft:** data field `cockpitStyle` (Godot `cockpit_style`): `'analog'` = grey plate, round dials (Super Mushshak); `'darkAnalog'` = round dials on a black-green plate, amber lettering, amber MACH / G strip (Mirage V); `'glass'` = F-16A/D: dark panel, two green MFD tapes (speed + Mach + G, altitude + VS bar), three gear lights, flap box. V/S dial range `gaugeVsiMaxFpm` (Mushshak 2000, jets 6000). Drawn by `src/ui/cockpit.js`, a straight port of `instrument_panel.gd` in its 266x114 units (restored 2026-11-19 at the owner's request: the JS port had one shared panel for all aircraft).
- **Sprite tool:** `python tools/make_aircraft_sprites.py [name]` (configs for super_mushshak and f16 inside). New aircraft = add a config + source picture, run it, copy the printed numbers into a new .tres, add the path to `GameState.AIRCRAFT`. Turn on mipmaps in each new PNG's .import.
- **Aircraft art (owner-approved approach):** the plane must look like the owner's art. Source picture: `docs/reference/super_mushshak_sprite_source.png` (plane on white). `python tools/make_aircraft_sprites.py` (needs Pillow) removes the white background and splits it into three aligned layers in `assets/sprites/`: `super_mushshak_body.png`, `_gear.png` (slides up behind the body to retract) and `_prop.png` (blurred blades, shown while the engine runs; a still blade is drawn when stopped). Textures are stored at 4x and drawn at `sprite_scale` 0.25 with mipmaps + linear filtering, so the plane looks smooth like the art, not blocky. On-screen length ~70 px ("original size" per owner; they rejected a 127 px version as too big, and rejected hand-made pixel sprites). The tool prints the numbers for the `sprite_*` fields in the .tres. Flaps are a small code-drawn red line at the wing trailing edge.
